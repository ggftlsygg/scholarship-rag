The American Legion Legacy Scholarship
About
The American Legion Legacy Scholarship is a needs-based scholarship designed to help cover the remaining gap in eligible educational expenses after federal and state grants, scholarships, and veterans' education benefits, if applicable, have been applied.
For the 2027-2028 academic year, tuition is capped at $30,908.43 which represents the maximum amount payable by the Department of Veterans Affairs (VA) per academic year.

What Can the Scholarship Be Used For?
The Legacy Scholarship provides needs-based financial assistance of up to $20,000 for eligible children of fallen service members and up to $5,000 for eligible children of disabled veterans. Scholarship funds may be used toward eligible undergraduate or graduate educational expenses at qualifying institutions within the United States.
Eligible recipients may reapply for additional annual scholarship awards up to six (6) times, provided they continue to meet the program's eligibility requirements and demonstrate qualifying financial need. Each additional award requires a new application and is subject to the program's current eligibility and financial review requirements.
Eligibility Details
Children of Fallen Post-9/11 Servicemembers
Eligible Requirements:
Must be the biological child, legally adopted child, stepchild, child of a spouse from a prior marriage, or dependent child as defined by the U.S. Armed Forces of a U.S. military service member who died while serving on active duty on or after September 11, 2001. Eligible service members include active-duty military personnel and National Guard members or military reservists who were federally activated at the time of death.
Must be a high school senior or graduate who has been accepted to or is currently attending an accredited institution of higher education within the United States.
Required Documentation:
Official military death certificate (DD Form 1300).
FAFSA Submission Summary showing the current Student Aid Index (SAI). For the 2024 application year, the FAFSA Expected Family Contribution (EFC) will be accepted.
If applicable, award notifications for federal or state veterans' education benefits (e.g., Marine Gunnery Sergeant John David Fry Scholarship, GI Bill benefits).
Financial aid award letter from the applicant’s prospective or current institution for the upcoming academic year.
Itemized breakdown of educational expenses, including, but not limited to, tuition, fees, books, program costs, meal plans, housing, and qualifying transportation expenses.
Children of Post-9/11 Veterans with a VA Disability Rating of 50% or Higher
Eligibility Requirements:
Must be biological child, legally adopted child, stepchild, child of a spouse from a prior marriage, or dependent child as defined by the U.S. Armed Forces of a U.S. military service member who sustained an injury while serving on active duty on or after September 11, 2001, resulting in a combined Department of Veterans Affairs (VA) disability rating of 50% or higher. Eligible service members include active-duty military personnel and National Guard members or military reservists who were federally activated at the time of injury.
Must be a high school senior or graduate who has been accepted to or is currently attending an accredited institution of higher education within the United States.
Required Documentation:
Official VA disability rating documentation showing a combined disability rating of 50% or higher.
FAFSA Submission Summary showing only the current Student Aid Index (SAI).
If applicable, any award notifications for federal or state veterans' education benefits (e.g., GI-Bill benefits).
Financial aid award letter from the applicant's prospective or current institution for the upcoming school year.
Itemized breakdown of education expenses, including, but not limited to, tuition, fees, books, program costs, meal plans, housing and qualifying transportation expenses.
DD Form 214 and/or NGB Form 22 showing dates of military service and character of discharge.
